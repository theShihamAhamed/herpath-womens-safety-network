# HerPath Usability Testing Baseline

## Objective

Evaluate whether people can complete safety-related tasks confidently and interpret the information without technical explanation from the development team. Testing should happen throughout development.

Recruit participants with variation in age, travel habits, digital confidence, navigation-app familiarity, and day/night travel patterns. Do not treat one participant type as representative of every user.

## Core tasks

### Map and route

Ask a participant leaving campus for an unfamiliar destination to find and choose a route. Observe destination-search discovery, From/To understanding, safety-context recognition, route comparison, and explanation of their choice.

### Map safety context

Ask the participant to check for recent reports around an area. Observe marker/filter/list understanding and whether they misinterpret no reports or limited data as safety.

### Report

Ask the participant to report an incident from yesterday, first with an exact point kept private and then with only an approximate area. Observe whether they understand permission timing, what the server stores, what the public Map shows, category/severity selection, editable time, identifying-information guidance, review, retry behavior, confirmation, and My Reports. Confirm they do not interpret the H3 center or public area as an exact incident point and do not interpret `PUBLISHED_UNVERIFIED` or community support as proof.

### Journey

Ask the participant to start a journey and show what they would do at arrival. Observe tracking consent, check-in understanding, and explicit safe-arrival confirmation.

### Safety Updates

Ask the participant to find recent safety updates. Observe whether the bell is discoverable and categories are understandable.

### Moderator

Ask an eligible participant to review a flagged incident. Observe flag reasons, privacy and duplicate context, decision confidence, and audit feedback.

## Recording

For each task record completion, delay, wrong taps/backtracking, hesitation, terminology or privacy misunderstanding, safety-label interpretation, participant comments, help requested, and expected next action.

Post-task prompts can ask what was expected next, what was unclear, what “Lower reported-risk” meant, whether location use was understood, and what should change.

## Assessment evidence

Retain the test plan, non-identifying participant summaries, observations, prioritized issues, before/after screenshots, and change rationale.

Strong evidence follows:

```text
research assumption → prototype/implementation → observed usability problem
→ design change → improved result
```
